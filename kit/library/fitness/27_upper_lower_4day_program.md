# The evidence-based 4-day upper/lower split program

**This complete program delivers two ready-to-use training templates — beginner and intermediate — built on current hypertrophy and strength research.** Both variations use a daily undulating periodization (DUP) model: "A" days emphasize heavier, strength-oriented work (lower reps, higher loads), while "B" days emphasize hypertrophy-oriented work (moderate loads, higher reps). Every exercise uses only barbells, dumbbells, cables, and a pull-up bar. The program includes RPE-based autoregulation, structured weekly progression, a deload protocol every 4th week, and warm-up guidance grounded in peer-reviewed evidence from Schoenfeld, Helms, Israetel, Nuckols, and Zourdos.

---

## How the program is designed and why it works

The upper/lower 4-day split trains every muscle group **twice per week** — the evidence-based minimum frequency for maximizing hypertrophy (Schoenfeld et al., 2016 meta-analysis). Sessions alternate between upper- and lower-body days on a Monday/Tuesday/Thursday/Friday schedule (or any arrangement providing 48+ hours between same-type sessions). A rest day separates each pair.

**Volume targets** draw from Renaissance Periodization's landmark system. Beginners start near MEV (Minimum Effective Volume) to build work capacity and technique. Intermediates operate within MAV (Maximum Adaptive Volume), ramping sets across a 3-week block before deloading. The table below shows weekly set targets per muscle group for each variation:

| Muscle group | Beginner (sets/week) | Intermediate (sets/week) | Research MAV range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 10 | 14–16 | 12–20 |
| Back | 10–12 | 16–18 | 14–22 |
| Quads | 8–10 | 14–16 | 12–18 |
| Hamstrings | 6–8 | 10–12 | 10–16 |
| Glutes | 4–6 (indirect) | 6–8 | 4–12 |
| Side delts | 6–8 | 10–12 | 16–22 |
| Rear delts | 4–6 | 6–8 | 12–18 |
| Biceps | 6–8 | 10–12 | 14–20 |
| Triceps | 6–8 | 8–10 | 10–14 |
| Calves | 6–8 | 8–10 | 8–16 |
| Abs | 4–6 | 6–8 | 10–16 |

Front delts receive zero direct sets because barbell and dumbbell pressing movements provide ample indirect volume. The intermediate variation sits at the lower-to-mid range of MAV intentionally — this leaves room to add **1–2 sets per muscle group per week** across the mesocycle's three hard weeks before deloading.

**Rest periods** follow Schoenfeld et al. (2016) and Wolf et al. (2024): **3–4 minutes** for heavy compound lifts (RPE 8–9, sets of 3–6 reps), **2–3 minutes** for moderate compound and accessory work, and **60–90 seconds** for isolation exercises.

## The RPE system and how to use it

Every working set in this program uses an RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) target mapped to Reps in Reserve (RIR), validated by Zourdos et al. (2016):

| RPE | Reps in reserve | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | 4 RIR | Moderate effort; clearly could do 4 more reps |
| **7** | 3 RIR | Solid effort; 3 good reps left in the tank |
| **8** | 2 RIR | Hard; could do 2 more reps with good form |
| **9** | 1 RIR | Very hard; only 1 more rep possible |
| **10** | 0 RIR | Maximum effort; absolute failure |

**Beginners should train mostly at RPE 6–8** (2–4 RIR). Research by Zourdos et al. shows novice lifters underestimate RIR by roughly 1–2 reps, so staying further from failure builds calibration skill while still driving adaptation. **Intermediates operate at RPE 7–9** (1–3 RIR), progressing RPE across weeks within the mesocycle. Compound lifts stay at RPE 7–8.5 to manage systemic fatigue; isolation lifts can reach RPE 9–10 (particularly on final sets) because failure on small-muscle exercises generates minimal recovery cost.

## Intermediate variation (18+ months training experience)

This variation uses barbell-dominant compounds, higher volume, more exercises per session, and an RPE ramp across the mesocycle. Progression follows **mesocycle periodization**: increase RPE by ~1 point per week across 3 hard weeks (Week 1 at RPE 7, Week 2 at RPE 8, Week 3 at RPE 8.5–9), then deload in Week 4. Add **1 set per muscle group in Weeks 2 and 3** to create progressive volume overload. Load increases weekly where possible (2.5 kg upper, 2.5–5 kg lower on compounds) and via double progression on accessories.

## The 4-week mesocycle structure with built-in deload

The program operates in repeating 4-week blocks: three progressively harder training weeks followed by one deload week. This structure aligns with the Delphi consensus on deloading (Bell et al., 2023) and RP's mesocycle model.

**Week 1 (introduction):** Use the prescribed sets at the lower RPE targets shown in the tables. This week should feel manageable — almost easy. The goal is to establish technique, find working weights, and begin building volume tolerance. Intermediates start at Week 1 RPE values; beginners hold RPE 7 throughout.

**Week 2 (development):** Add **1 set to each exercise** that targets a priority muscle group (compounds and 1–2 isolations). Increase load by 2.5 kg on barbell compounds where RPE allows. Intermediates push RPE to ~8 on compounds, ~8.5 on isolations.

**Week 3 (overreach):** Add another set to 2–3 exercises (focus on compound lifts). Push RPE to 8.5–9 on compounds and 9–9.5 on isolations. This is the hardest week — you should feel fatigued by Friday. Beginners stay at RPE 8 maximum.

**Week 4 (deload):** The deload week is critical for dissipating accumulated fatigue and allowing supercompensation. Follow these specific reductions:

- **Cut total sets by 50%** — perform only 2 sets per exercise instead of 3–4
- **Maintain the same exercises** and training frequency (still train 4 days)
- **Use Week 1 loads** — do not increase weight
- **Drop reps to roughly 50% of normal** (e.g., if you did sets of 10, do sets of 5)
- **RPE should stay at 5–6** — nothing should feel challenging
- **Keep rest periods shorter** (2 min maximum) — sessions should take 25–35 minutes

After the deload, begin a new mesocycle. Increase Week 1 starting loads by **2.5 kg on upper body compounds and 5 kg on lower body compounds** compared to the previous mesocycle's Week 1. Restart the RPE and volume ramp from scratch.

## Warm-up protocol for every session

**General warm-up (5–8 minutes):** Light cardio — brisk incline walking, jumping jacks, or jump rope — until you break a light sweat. Follow with 2–3 minutes of dynamic movement prep: leg swings (front/back and side-to-side), arm circles, hip circles, and bodyweight squats. Avoid aggressive static stretching before lifting; short holds under 30 seconds are acceptable only for muscles limiting your range of motion.

**Specific warm-up (ramping sets) before the first compound lift of the session:**

| Warm-up set | Reps | Load (% of first working set) |
|---|---|---|
| Set 1 | 8–10 | Empty bar or ~40% |
| Set 2 | 5 | ~60% |
| Set 3 | 3 | ~80% |
| Set 4 (optional, for heavy days) | 1–2 | ~90% |

For the **second compound exercise** targeting the same muscle group pattern, perform 1–2 lighter sets. For **isolation exercises**, warm-up sets are unnecessary — the muscle is already warm from compound work. One "feeler" set of 5 easy reps is fine if you want to establish the mind-muscle connection.

## Progression schemes that match your training level

**Beginner progression** operates on two tracks. For **barbell compounds** (bench press, squat, RDL), use straight linear progression: add **2.5 kg (5 lb) per week** on upper body lifts and **2.5–5 kg (5–10 lb) per week** on lower body lifts. When you can no longer add weight while staying within the rep range at the target RPE, reset the weight by 10% and build back up. For **dumbbell and cable exercises**, use double progression: work within the prescribed rep range (e.g., 10–12), add reps each session until hitting the top of the range across all sets, then increase weight by the smallest available increment and restart at the bottom of the range.

**Intermediate progression** uses the mesocycle ramp described above. Within each 3-week hard block, increase RPE and volume weekly. Load increases happen **when the target RPE drops below the prescribed range** — meaning the weight has become too easy at the intended effort level. A practical heuristic: if you hit the top of your rep range at RPE 7 when the target is RPE 8, add 2.5 kg next session. Between mesocycles, increase starting loads by small increments. Isolation exercises continue using double progression regardless of training level because fixed-weight dumbbells make percentage-based loading impractical.

**When to transition from beginner to intermediate programming:** Stop linear progression when you fail to add weight for **two consecutive sessions** on a lift despite adequate sleep, nutrition, and a deload. This typically happens after **6–12 months** of consistent training.

## Conclusion: building long-term progress from this template

This program provides a complete, scalable system — not just a workout list. The beginner variation builds movement competency and work capacity with conservative volume (**~35–45 working sets per week total**) and straightforward linear progression. The intermediate variation drives continued adaptation through periodized volume and intensity ramps (**~55–70 working sets per week**), DUP-style rep range variation between A and B days, and autoregulated RPE targets that account for daily readiness.

Three principles matter more than any specific exercise choice. First, **progressive overload must happen** — through more weight, more reps, or more sets over time. Second, **proximity to failure drives hypertrophy** — research consistently shows sets taken within 0–4 RIR stimulate growth, while sets stopped at 5+ RIR contribute very little. Third, **fatigue management determines longevity** — the deload week is not optional. Skipping it leads to accumulated fatigue that eventually stalls or reverses progress. Track your RPE honestly, deload proactively, and trust that easier weeks make harder weeks possible. After running 3–4 full mesocycles (12–16 weeks), reassess your volume needs, swap exercises that feel stale or painful, and consider advancing priority muscle groups toward the upper end of their MAV range while keeping non-priority groups closer to maintenance.
