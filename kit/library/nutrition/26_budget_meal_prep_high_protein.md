# The $65 high-protein meal prep playbook

**You can hit 150g of protein daily on roughly $65 a week** by building meals around five budget powerhouses: bone-in chicken thighs, eggs, dried lentils, Greek yogurt, and ground turkey. This guide provides a complete, copy-paste-ready system — shopping list, batch cooking workflow, a full 7-day meal plan with gram-level portions, and swap tables for vegetarian, dairy-free, and gluten-free diets. Every meal and snack below has been costed against 2025–2026 US grocery prices, which currently favor chicken and pork over beef (ground beef hit a record **$6.75/lb** in January 2026, making it the worst protein value on shelves).

The core strategy is simple: spend two to three hours on Sunday preparing five base components — slow-cooker shredded chicken, sheet-pan chicken thighs, turkey chili, egg muffins, and a pot of rice and lentils — then rotate sauces and toppings throughout the week so nothing tastes repetitive. The math works out to roughly **$1.50–$2.50 per meal** and **2,100–2,400 calories per day** with **152–168g protein**.

## Sunday batch cook: a 2.5-hour workflow

The entire week's food preps in a single session. The key is running the oven, stovetop, and slow cooker simultaneously. Here is the minute-by-minute flow.

**Phase 1 (0–10 min): Start passive cooking.** Place **3 lbs chicken thighs** (bone-in, seasoned with 1 tsp each paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, salt, pepper, and 1 tbsp oil) on a parchment-lined sheet pan and slide into a **425°F oven** for 30 minutes. Set a large pot of **3 cups dry rice + 4.5 cups water** on the stovetop, bring to a boil, cover, reduce to low. Place **8 eggs** in a pot, cover with cold water, bring to a boil, then cover and set a 12-minute timer for hard-boiled eggs.

**Phase 2 (10–30 min): Prep while things cook.** Dice 2 onions, mince 4 garlic cloves, chop 1 bell pepper. Measure out lentils and chili spices. Mix the egg muffin batter: whisk **10 eggs + ½ cup cottage cheese + salt + pepper** in a bowl; dice remaining bell pepper and thaw ½ bag frozen spinach for the filling. Brown **2 lbs ground turkey** in a large Dutch oven, breaking into crumbles (8 minutes). Drain hard-boiled eggs into ice water.

**Phase 3 (30–60 min): Chili and lentils go on, chicken comes out.** To the browned turkey, add diced onion, bell pepper, and garlic — sauté 4 minutes. Add **2 cans black beans** (drained), **2 cans diced tomatoes**, **1 can tomato sauce**, 1 cup water, 2 tbsp chili powder, 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tsp paprika, ½ tsp oregano. Stir, bring to a boil, reduce heat and simmer 30 minutes. In a separate pot, bring **2 cups dried lentils + 5 cups water** to a boil, reduce and simmer 25 minutes. Remove the sheet pan chicken from the oven, rest 5 minutes, then portion into containers. Reduce oven to **375°F**.

**Phase 4 (60–90 min): Egg muffins bake, oats assembled.** Grease a 12-cup silicone muffin tin. Distribute sautéed spinach and peppers, pour egg-cottage cheese batter over filling. Bake at 375°F for 20 minutes. While they bake, prepare **5 jars of overnight oats**: in each jar combine ½ cup oats, ½ cup Greek yogurt, ½ cup water, 1 tbsp peanut butter, 1 scoop whey protein, pinch of salt. Stir, cap, refrigerate.

**Phase 5 (90–150 min): Cool, portion, store.** Let all cooked food cool 15 minutes uncovered (sealing hot food traps steam and makes everything soggy). Portion into containers by meal. The week's containers should look like this:

- **7 overnight oat jars** (breakfast, Mon–Sun)
- **12 egg muffins** in a single container (breakfast/snack add-on)
- **~3.5 lbs cooked chicken thigh meat** in 7 portions (~8 oz each)
- **8 servings turkey chili** in individual containers
- **~12 cups cooked rice** portioned into 7 containers (~1.5 cups each)
- **~6 cups cooked lentils** portioned into 4 containers (~1.5 cups each)
- **8 hard-boiled eggs** in a container

Store days 1–4 in the fridge; freeze days 5–7 and thaw overnight the evening before.

## Five sauces that make the same chicken taste different every day

Repetitive base ingredients need a rotating cast of sauces. Each takes under five minutes to make, costs less than $0.50 per serving, and keeps 7–10 days in the fridge. Make two per week.

**Spicy peanut sauce** works on chicken-rice bowls and stir-fries: whisk 3 tbsp peanut butter, 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tsp sriracha, and 2 tbsp warm water. **Quick teriyaki glaze** transforms plain chicken into something you'd actually crave: combine ¼ cup soy sauce, 2 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tsp sesame oil, and 1 minced garlic clove in a small saucepan, thicken with 1 tsp cornstarch dissolved in 1 tbsp water, and simmer 2 minutes. **Greek yogurt ranch** doubles as a protein booster — stir 1 packet ranch seasoning into 1 cup Greek yogurt with 2 tbsp water for a dressing that adds **15g protein per half cup**. For a Mediterranean night, blend **lemon-herb tahini**: ¼ cup tahini, juice of 1 lemon, 1 minced garlic clove, pinch of salt, and water to thin. And for something bright and herbaceous, pulse **chimichurri** in a food processor: 1 cup parsley, ¼ cup olive oil, 2 tbsp red wine vinegar, 3 garlic cloves, and ½ tsp red pepper flakes.

The rotation system means Monday's chicken-rice-broccoli bowl with peanut sauce tastes completely different from Wednesday's version with teriyaki or Friday's with chimichurri — even though the base prep is identical.

## Vegetarian swaps that still hit 150g protein

Reaching **150g protein without meat** on this budget is achievable but requires deliberate food choices. The most cost-effective vegetarian proteins are **dried lentils (60–72g protein per dollar)**, **dried beans (50–64g per dollar)**, and **eggs (16–24g per dollar)**. Tofu, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, and whey protein round out the toolkit.

For every meat component in the plan above, here are direct swaps:

| Meat item | Vegetarian swap | Protein match |
|---|---|---|
| 8 oz chicken thighs (42g protein) | 1 block extra-firm tofu (20g) + 2 tbsp nutritional yeast (8g) + ½ cup lentils (9g) = 37g | 88% match |
| Turkey chili (2 lbs ground turkey) | 2 lbs crumbled extra-firm tofu + 1 extra can black beans — keep all other ingredients identical | ~28g/serving vs 32g |
| Hard-boiled eggs | Keep as-is (lacto-ovo vegetarian) | 100% match |
| Protein shake (whey) | Keep whey, or swap to pea protein isolate (20–24g/scoop, ~$1.00–$1.50/serving) | 80–100% match |

A sample vegetarian day that hits **157g protein**: breakfast of overnight oats with whey and yogurt (33g), mid-morning snack of 3 egg muffins (24g), lunch of lentil dal with rice and a side of Greek yogurt (22g + 17g = 39g), afternoon snack of cottage cheese and edamame (14g + 12g = 26g), dinner of tofu stir-fry over rice with peanut sauce (20g + 5g + 4g = 29g), and an evening protein shake (25g). **Budget impact: saves $3–5/week** since tofu ($2–3/block) and lentils ($1.50/lb) are cheaper than chicken.

## Dairy-free adjustments without sacrificing protein

Going dairy-free removes Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, and whey protein — three workhorses in this plan. The honest reality is that **most dairy-free alternatives carry far less protein**. Almond milk has just 1–2g per cup versus cow's milk at 8g. Dairy-free cheese is essentially protein-free. Coconut-based yogurt averages a dismal 1.2g protein per 100g.

The viable replacements:

- **Greek yogurt → Silk soy yogurt** (6–7g/serving, ~$1.50) or **Kite Hill Greek-style almond yogurt** (17g/serving, ~$2.50–3.00, though expensive). Alternatively, blend silken tofu with vanilla and sweetener for a yogurt-like base at a fraction of the cost.
- **Whey protein → Pea protein isolate** (20–27g/scoop). NOW Sports Pea Protein costs ~$0.80–1.00/serving in bulk. This is the closest nutritional match.
- **Cottage cheese → Crumbled extra-firm tofu** seasoned with salt and nutritional yeast (10g protein per ½ cup, ~$0.75/serving). Similar crumbly texture, much cheaper than specialty dairy-free cottage cheese.
- **Milk → Unsweetened soy milk** (7–8g/cup, ~$3.50/half gallon). The USDA considers fortified soy milk nutritionally equivalent to cow's milk. Avoid oat and almond milk for protein purposes.

**Budget impact: adds roughly $5–10/week** due to pricier protein powder and yogurt alternatives. Offset this by replacing specialty dairy-free products with whole-food swaps (tofu costs less than dairy-free yogurt and delivers more protein).

## Gluten-free modifications that won't blow the budget

Gluten-free specialty products carry an average **183% price premium** — GF bread costs $5.50–8.00 per loaf versus $1.70 for regular wheat bread. The budget-smart approach is to avoid GF specialty products almost entirely and lean into **naturally gluten-free starches** that cost the same or less than their wheat counterparts.

| Standard item | Gluten-free swap | Price impact |
|---|---|---|
| Whole wheat bread ($1.68/loaf) | Corn tortillas ($1.50–2.00/30 ct) | **Saves money** |
| Regular pasta ($0.92/lb) | White rice (already in plan, $0.80–0.92/lb) or Banza chickpea pasta ($3.29/box, 25g protein/serving) | Neutral to +$2.50 |
| Old-fashioned oats | Certified GF oats (Bob's Red Mill, $4.99–5.99/32 oz) | +$2.00/week |
| Breadcrumbs in meatballs | Crushed GF crackers or almond flour | +$0.50/batch |
| Soy sauce | Tamari (naturally GF, same price) | Neutral |

The total gluten-free adjustment adds only **$2–5/week** when you build around rice, potatoes, corn tortillas, and sweet potatoes rather than buying GF bread and pasta. One power move: **Banza chickpea pasta** pulls double duty as both a GF starch and a protein booster at **25g protein per serving** versus 7g for rice pasta.

## Twelve rules for stretching every grocery dollar

Smart shopping can shave 15–30% off your weekly spend without changing what you eat. The highest-impact strategies:

- **Shop primarily at Aldi**, which beat Walmart on 24 of 29 comparable grocery items in 2025 testing. Supplement with monthly Costco trips for bulk eggs, protein powder, rice, and frozen vegetables — Consumer Reports found Costco **21.4% cheaper than Walmart** overall.
- **Buy store brand everything.** Great Value, Kirkland, and Aldi private-label products save **20–40%** over name brands with equivalent quality for staples like rice, oats, beans, frozen vegetables, and canned goods.
- **Stack savings apps.** Use Ibotta (select offers before shopping, scan receipt after) and Fetch Rewards (scan any receipt for points) on the same trip. Layer store digital coupons and a cashback credit card on top for a "quadruple dip" that realistically saves **$5–15/week**.
- **Shop Wednesday mornings** for the overlap of new weekly specials and lingering previous-week deals. Meat markdowns of 30–50% happen most frequently on Monday mornings and Thursday evenings.
- **Avoid ground beef** at its current record price. Bone-in chicken thighs deliver **3× more protein per dollar** than 80/20 ground beef in 2026 pricing.
- **Freeze strategically.** Portion bulk-bought chicken into meal-sized bags before freezing. Freeze bread (toast slices directly from frozen). Cook a double batch of chili and freeze half. Label everything with dates.

## Conclusion

This system works because it treats protein as the fixed constraint and builds everything else around it. The five-component Sunday prep — shredded chicken, roasted thighs, turkey chili, egg muffins, and lentils-plus-rice — generates **21 meals and 14 snacks** from roughly two and a half hours of cooking and $65 in groceries. The sauce rotation prevents palate fatigue without adding meaningful cost. And because the base plan relies on chicken, eggs, lentils, and dairy rather than beef, it's structurally resistant to the beef-price inflation currently hitting US grocery stores.

The dietary restriction swaps are designed to preserve both the protein target and the budget. Vegetarians actually spend less by substituting tofu and extra lentils. Dairy-free eaters should anchor on pea protein and soy milk rather than expensive specialty products. Gluten-free eaters save the most by embracing rice, potatoes, and corn tortillas instead of paying the GF bread tax. In every case, the underlying architecture — batch-cooked base proteins, portioned starches, rotating sauces — stays the same. Only the ingredients change.
