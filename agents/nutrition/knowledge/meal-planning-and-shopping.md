# Meal planning and budget shopping

## Plan first, then shop
- Plan before shopping, to save time and money. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:60
- Nutrition.gov offers a sample 7-day Mediterranean meal plan, as an example, not a prescription. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:64
- It offers a weekly meal planner with a grocery list, a weekly menu planner, and a seasonal produce guide. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:67
- "Shop with Meals in Mind" uses tracking lists to cut food waste. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:69
- A meal prep guide for college students is the closest federal guide to batch cooking. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:71

## The SNAP-Ed order for a tight budget
- 1. Plan to use the food already at home first. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:76
- 2. Know how much money there is for food. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:77
- 3. Make the list from that budget and what the planned meals need. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:78
- 4. Buy only what can be used before it spoils. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:79
- SNAP-Ed calls this skill set Food Resource Management. [^74] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:82
- The shopping list is made from the meal plan, never separately. That is also what prevents waste. [^73] [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:86

## Reference budgets
- USDA publishes four food plans at rising cost: Thrifty, Low-Cost, Moderate-Cost and Liberal. [^76] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:94
- Each is re-priced every month with the Consumer Price Index (CPI-U). [^76] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:95
- The monthly reports give costs by age and sex, weekly and monthly. [^76] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:96
- Never hard-code a grocery budget figure: it goes stale within weeks. This agent has no current figure and says so. [^76] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:101

## What healthy food costs (no figures kept here; the book has them)
- USDA ERS priced 155 fruits and vegetables in cup equivalents at 2022 national average prices. [^77] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:105
- Meeting the fruit and vegetable recommendations was possible at a low daily cost in 2022 prices. [^77] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:106
- Of 93 vegetables, 19 fell in the cheapest price band, including baked white potato, iceberg lettuce and onions. [^78] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:109
- The cheapest items per cup equivalent were fresh watermelon and dried pinto beans. [^78] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:110

## Shopping tactics
- Use unit pricing to compare products, alongside the Nutrition Facts label. [^75] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:118
- Use all forms of produce: fresh, frozen and canned. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:120
- Buy what is in season or on sale. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:121
- Frozen and canned fruit is available all year, can save money, and has similar nutrition to fresh. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:122
- Watch sodium (and sugar) in canned food. [^54] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:124

## The weekly workflow
- 1. Inventory first: plan to eat what is already at home. [^73] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:194
- 2. Set the budget, against the current USDA food plan for the household. [^73] [^76] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:195
- 3. Choose the pattern: DASH- or Mediterranean-shaped unless a condition says otherwise. [^13] [^5] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:196
- 4. Build the menu, then derive the grocery list from it. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:197
- 5. Price the produce: in season, on sale, frozen or canned where cheaper. [^73] [^77] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:198
- 6. Lean on legumes: dried pinto beans were the cheapest cup equivalent in the ERS data. [^78] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:199
- 7. Shop with unit prices and the label's 5%/20% DV rule. [^75] [^10] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:200
- 8. Batch cook, then chill fast: out of the danger zone within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F). [^69] [^67] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:201
- 9. Cook to temperature with a thermometer. [^68] [^67] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:202
- 10. Label and date leftovers: 3-4 days in the fridge; reheat to 165°F. [^71] [^68] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:203
- 11. Freeze the overflow at 0°F or below before day five. [^67] [^70] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:204
- 12. Close the loop: tracking lists to cut waste next time. [^72] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:205
