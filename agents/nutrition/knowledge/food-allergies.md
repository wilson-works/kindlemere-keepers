# Food allergies and intolerances

## The nine major US allergens and the label law
- The nine: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame. [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:182
- Sesame became the ninth under the FASTER Act and must be labelled from 1 January 2023. [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:183
- Makers must declare it in one of three places: the ingredient list by common name, a "Contains" statement, or in brackets after the ingredient. [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:184
- "May contain" statements are not covered by the mandatory rule. [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:185

## How common
- Convincing food allergy in US adults: about 10.8%. [^42] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:189
- In children: about 8.0%. [^43] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:190
- Most common in adults: shellfish 2.9%, milk 1.9%, peanut 1.8%, tree nut 1.2%. [^42] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:191

## Preventing peanut allergy: early, not avoidance
- NIAID published its peanut-prevention addendum in January 2017, after the LEAP trial. [^44] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:196
- LEAP enrolled 640 infants aged 4-11 months with severe eczema, egg allergy, or both. [^45] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:197
- Infants who ate peanut through age 5 had an 81% lower risk of peanut allergy. [^45] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:198
- Introduce peanut foods to all infants before age 1, from about 4-6 months, once they can manage solids. [^45] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:199
- Infants with moderate-to-severe eczema or egg allergy are highest risk and are tested first; others need no test. [^45] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:200
- The agent explains the guideline. It must refuse to direct an at-home introduction for a high-risk infant, and route to an allergist. [^45] [^44] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:204

## Lactose intolerance: an intolerance, not an allergy
- Many people with lactose intolerance can have 12 g lactose, about 1 cup of milk, without real symptoms. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:208
- Small portions with meals, gradual reintroduction, and yogurt or hard cheeses (cheddar, Swiss), which are lower in lactose. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:209
- Lactase enzyme products widen tolerance. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:210
- Hidden lactose: bread, baked goods, processed foods, cereals, processed meats, coffee creamers. Label words: milk, whey, curds, dry milk solids. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:211
- Non-dairy calcium: fish with soft bones, leafy greens, broccoli, oranges, almonds, fortified products. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:212
- Default to finding the dose, not elimination, and contrast it with milk allergy, where strict avoidance applies. [^46] [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:216

## Swapping safely: the procedure at the stove
- First decide: allergy (strict avoidance plus cross-contact control) or intolerance (dose management). [^41] [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:148
- For each of the nine allergens, check all three declaration places. [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:149
- Re-check on every purchase. "May contain" is outside the mandatory rule and no substitute for it. [^41] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:150
- Gluten: use products labelled gluten-free (legally under 20 ppm); buy oats labelled gluten-free. [^28] [^30] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:151
- Control cross-contact with separate equipment by default. [^31] [^32] [^33] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:153
- Lactose: swap toward lower-lactose dairy or lactase, not full elimination. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:154
- When dairy comes out, replace the calcium. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:155
- Scan for hidden lactose words in bread, cereals, processed meats and creamers. [^46] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:156
