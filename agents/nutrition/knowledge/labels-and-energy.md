# Labels, energy needs, processing

## Reading a Nutrition Facts label
- 5% DV or less per serving is low; 20% DV or more is high. [^10] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:170
- Nutrients to get less of: saturated fat, sodium, added sugars. [^10] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:171
- Nutrients to get more of: dietary fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron, potassium. [^10] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:172
- Serving size describes typical eating. It is not a recommendation. [^10] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:173

## Estimating energy needs (Mifflin-St Jeor)
- The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics: measure resting metabolic rate (RMR) by indirect calorimetry if possible; if not, use Mifflin-St Jeor with actual body weight. A Strong (Conditional) recommendation. [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:183
- Men: RMR = (9.99 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (4.92 × age) + 5. [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:185
- Women: RMR = (9.99 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (4.92 × age) − 161. [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:186
- In people with obesity it lands within 10% of measured RMR in about 70%, overestimating up to 9% and underestimating up to 21%. [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:190
- It is less accurate in obese than non-obese adults (about 75% against 87% in one analysis). [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:191
- It was not validated in racial groups other than White participants. [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:192
- Give a range with the error band (up to 9% over, 21% under). Never a single calorie number: the false precision is the harm. [^9] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:196

## Processing changes how much people eat
- In a 4-week inpatient crossover trial (n=20), diets matched for presented calories, sugar, fat, sodium, fiber and macros still diverged: intake was 508 ± 106 kcal a day higher on the ultra-processed diet. [^15] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:200
- People gained 0.9 kg on the ultra-processed diet and lost 0.9 kg on the unprocessed one. [^15] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:201
- The extra came from carbohydrate and fat, not protein. [^15] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:202
- NIH calls it the first RCT directly comparing intake and weight on ultra-processed against unprocessed diets. [^16] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:204
- Asked "calories or food quality?": answer with this trial. Processing changed spontaneous intake by about 500 kcal a day at matched macros. [^15] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:208
- The 2025-2030 DGA is vague on what counts as a problem processed food, so describe processing concretely: packaged, ready-to-eat, multi-ingredient, industrially formulated. [^3] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:186
