# Celiac disease and gluten

## The thresholds, kept apart
- FDA's rule of 2 August 2013: a food may say "gluten-free" if it has under 20 ppm gluten. [^28] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:94
- 20 ppm was chosen because methods could not reliably detect gluten below it. [^28] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:95
- About 10 mg of gluten a day is safe for the vast majority of people with celiac disease. [^29] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:96
- Celiac disease affects about 1 in 100 people worldwide. [^29] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:98
- Keep the legal threshold (20 ppm), the body's threshold (about 10 mg a day) and kitchen evidence apart. Mixing them causes fear or false safety. [^28] [^29] [^32] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:115

## Where gluten hides
- Gluten comes from wheat, barley and rye, and turns up in many non-obvious products, so label reading is the core skill. [^30] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:102
- Oats are easily contaminated: buy oats labelled gluten-free. [^30] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:103
- "Wheat-free" is not "gluten-free": it does not cover barley or rye. [^30] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:152

## The kitchen
- Cross-contact is when a gluten-free food meets a gluten-containing ingredient, making it unsafe. [^31] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:104
- Separate equipment is the conventional advice, including a dedicated toaster. [^31] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:105
- A preliminary study found no significant gluten transfer from a shared toaster (stayed under 20 ppm). [^32] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:109
- A pilot study of shared fryers is the source for restaurant fryer questions. [^33] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:110
- Separate equipment stays the default; measured studies can refine it, not replace it. [^31] [^32] [^33] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/04-cooking-meal-prep-budget.md:153

## Where the agent stops
- May teach label reading and kitchen separation. [^34] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:119
- Diagnosis needs blood tests and a biopsy before gluten comes out. Never advise starting gluten-free before testing; route to a gastroenterologist. [^34] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:119
- NIDDK's guidance covers the practical diet and recommends a dietitian. [^34] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/02-medical-diets-allergies.md:111
