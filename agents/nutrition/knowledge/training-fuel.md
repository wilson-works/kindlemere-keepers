# Fuel and fluid for training

## What this agent owns, and what the fitness coach owns
- This agent owns energy and macronutrient needs as a function of training load. [^81] [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:14
- It owns protein amount, per-meal dose and spread. [^83] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:15
- It owns carbohydrate before, during and after exercise. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:16
- It owns fluid and electrolyte arithmetic. [^85] [^86] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:17
- It owns energy availability and the nutrition side of REDs screening and referral. [^87] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:18
- It owns caffeine as a food compound, including its safe ceiling. [^17] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:19
- The fitness coach owns sets and reps, session design, periodisation, progression, technique, injury and return to play. Training volume is an input here, never an output. [^81] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:23
- When fixing a nutrition problem needs a change to the training, state the nutrition finding and hand the training decision to the coach. [^87] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:27

## Carbohydrate per day by training load
- Carbohydrate for moderate exercise, about 1 hour a day: 5-7 g/kg/day. [^81] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:74
- Carbohydrate for moderate-to-high intensity, 1-3 hours a day: 6-10 g/kg/day. [^81] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:75
- Carbohydrate, general athlete range (ISSN): 5-12 g/kg/day. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:76
- Carbohydrate for heavy weekly volume, to fill glycogen: about 8-12 g/kg/day. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:78

## During and after
- Sessions longer than 60-90 minutes: about 30-60 g carbohydrate an hour in a 6-8% drink, 6-12 fl oz every 10-15 minutes. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:82
- Next session under 4 hours away: 1.2 g/kg an hour to restore glycogen fast. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:87
- Or 0.6-1.0 g/kg in the first 30 minutes, then again every 2 hours for 4-6 hours. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:88
- Below 1.2 g/kg an hour of carbohydrate, adding 0.2-0.4 g/kg an hour of protein helps glycogen recovery. [^84] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:89
- The ISSN sports caffeine dose (3-8 mg/kg) can exceed the FDA's 400 mg a day for healthy adults: 8 mg/kg for an 80 kg athlete is 640 mg. Always name that conflict, and give no sports caffeine dose under 18. [^84] [^17] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:93 @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:189

## Fluid and electrolytes
- Everyday total water: 3.7 L a day for men, 2.7 L for women, counting food. [^6] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:101
- Those assume light-to-moderate activity in a temperate climate, so training needs its own sum. [^6] [^85] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:103
- Replace sweat losses at the rate you sweat them. [^85] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:107
- ACSM: 0.4-0.8 L an hour is optimal in intense endurance work. [^85] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:108
- Past 1 hour, add sodium at 0.5-0.7 g per litre of drink. [^85] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:109
- Sodium is needed when sweat rate is above 1.2 L an hour and the session lasts more than 2 hours. [^85] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:110
- Low blood sodium comes from drinking too much, not too little. "Drink as much as you can" is unsafe. [^85] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:115
- Ask duration, intensity and conditions before a fluid number. Never give an athlete one daily litre figure. [^85] [^6] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:119

## Energy availability and REDs
- The IOC's 2023 consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) is the reference. [^87] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:127
- REDs is a mismatch between calories eaten and calories burned in exercise, harming health and performance. [^88] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:128
- REDs is common in both men and women across many sports: never screen only women. [^88] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:130
- The 30 kcal/kg fat-free-mass cut-off came from 5-day studies of sedentary women. [^89] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:137
- The last IOC consensus abandoned cut-off values, yet practitioners still use 30 kcal/kg. [^89] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:140
- Use 30 kcal/kg only as a reason to ask more and consider referral, never as a diagnosis. [^89] [^87] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:145
- Low intake plus missed periods, stress fractures or falling performance is a referral, not a meal-plan problem. [^87] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/05-nutrition-exercise-interface.md:153
