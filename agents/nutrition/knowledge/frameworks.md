# Which guidance anchors the numbers

Every fact below has its page, its line and its footnote in the book. The footnote's full source is in the book's `sources.md`.

## The two US frameworks
- The Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs) come from the National Academies and are revised nutrient by nutrient, not on a fixed cycle. [^6] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:13
- The Dietary Guidelines for Americans (DGA) are the federal food-pattern policy, revised every 5 years by USDA and HHS. [^1] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:14
- The current edition is the 10th, Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025-2030, published January 2026, headline "Eat Real Food". [^1] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:15
- The full 2025-2030 text and scientific report live at RealFood.gov. [^2] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:16
- Name the edition and date whenever a DGA number is quoted. Any cached figure has a shelf life. [^1] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:21

## The 2025-2030 DGA is contested, so say so
- It promotes 1.2-1.6 g protein per kg per day, 50-100% above the 0.8 g/kg/day RDA. [^2] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:29
- It left the protein AMDR (10-35% of calories for adults) unchanged. [^6] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:30
- It keeps limits of under 10% of calories from saturated fat, under 10% from added sugars, and under 2,300 mg sodium a day. [^5] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:31
- Stanford's nutrition group holds that the protein target plus red meat, full-fat dairy, butter and beef tallow makes the saturated-fat ceiling hard or impossible to hit. [^3] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:32
- A published critical analysis calls this "a dietary paradox". [^4] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:33
- Stanford flagged factual errors: olive oil, butter and beef tallow listed as "healthy fats", and xylitol miscategorised. [^3] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:34
- Stanford notes fiber, which Americans under-eat, is under-emphasised. [^3] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:35
- Stanford raises an equity objection to the dairy emphasis, since about 75% of the world is lactose intolerant. [^3] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:36
- The DGA protein target conflicts with the ADA's about 0.8 g/kg/day for diabetes, and fewer than 15% of people with CKD know they have it. [^5] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:37
- When quoting a contested DGA figure, say so in one clause and name the disagreement. Do not arbitrate between USDA and the specialty societies. [^5] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:41

## WHO cross-check numbers
- Fruit and vegetables, adults 10+: at least 400 g a day. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:47
- Fruit and vegetables, children 6-9: at least 350 g a day; children 2-5: at least 250 g a day. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:48
- Naturally occurring fiber, adults: at least 25 g a day. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:49
- Free sugars: under 10% of energy, under 5% for added benefit. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:50
- Total fat at most 30% of energy. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:51
- Saturated fat at most 10% of energy. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:52
- Trans fat at most 1% of energy. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:53
- Salt under 5 g a day (about 2 g sodium). [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:54
- Potassium at least 90 mmol a day (3,510 mg). [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:55
- WHO sets protein at 10-15% of energy and carbohydrate at about 45-75%. [^11] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:57
