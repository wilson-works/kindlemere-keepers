# Portioning a plate or a prep box

Researched for Avo's kitchen, 2026-10-09 (lane AVO, n1009). One fact per line, in plain words, with its source and address.

- Start with a 9-inch plate. [^7] (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)
- Fill half the plate with non-starchy vegetables such as salad, green beans or broccoli. [^7] (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)
- Fill a quarter with lean protein such as chicken, beans, tofu or eggs. [^7] (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)
- Fill a quarter with carbohydrate foods: grains, starchy vegetables, rice, pasta, beans, fruit or yogurt. [^7] (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)
- Drink water or a low-calorie drink with the meal. [^7] (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)
- Eating about the same amount of carbohydrate at each meal can help steady blood sugar; the amount is for a doctor or dietitian to set. [^7] (https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html)
- The 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines (the New Pyramid) suggest 3 servings of vegetables and 2 of fruit a day. [^8] (https://realfood.gov/)
- They suggest 2 to 4 servings of whole grains a day. [^8] (https://realfood.gov/)
- The New Pyramid is a flexible framework for choices, not a set menu. [^8] (https://realfood.gov/)
- Hand portions: a palm of protein (about 3 to 4 oz cooked meat), a fist of vegetables (about 1 cup), a cupped hand of carbohydrate (about 1/2 to 2/3 cup cooked grains, or a medium fruit), a thumb of fat (about 1 tablespoon). [^16] (https://www.precisionnutrition.com/hand-portion-faq)
- A starting point is one of each hand portion per meal for women and two for men (carbs and fats at most meals), changed one portion at a time. [^17] (https://www.precisionnutrition.com/calorie-control-guide)
