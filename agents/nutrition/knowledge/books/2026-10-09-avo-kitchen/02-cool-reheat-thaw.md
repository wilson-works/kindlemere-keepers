# Cooling, reheating and thawing a batch safely

Researched for Avo's kitchen, 2026-10-09 (lane AVO, n1009). One fact per line, in plain words, with its source and address.

- Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour when it is above 90°F. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Throw away perishable food left at room temperature longer than that. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Bacteria grow fast between 40°F and 140°F; keep hot food at 140°F or warmer. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Cool a batch fast by splitting it into shallow containers: a big pot of soup cools too slowly. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Cut large items such as roasts into smaller pieces before chilling them. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Hot food can go straight into the fridge, or into an ice or cold-water bath first. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Cover, wrap airtight or seal leftovers in containers, then refrigerate or freeze them right away. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Reheat leftovers to 165°F on a food thermometer. [^3] [^5] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety ; https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html)
- Bring sauces, soups and gravies to a rolling boil when reheating them. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- In the microwave, cover and vent the dish, stir or rotate it, and check the temperature in several places: microwaves leave cold spots. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Frozen leftovers can be reheated without thawing: soups and stews in a pan or microwave, casseroles in the oven or microwave. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Cooking or reheating from frozen takes about one and a half times as long. [^4] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety)
- Thaw food in the fridge, in cold water or in the microwave; never on the counter. [^4] [^5] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety ; https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html)
- Fridge thawing is slowest but safe the whole time: small items thaw overnight, most foods take a day or two. [^4] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety)
- Cold-water thawing: seal the food in a leak-proof bag, change the water every 30 minutes, and cook it right after. [^4] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety)
- Cook food thawed in the microwave right away. [^4] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety)
- Leftovers thawed in the fridge keep 3 to 4 more days, or can be refrozen. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Food thawed in cold water or the microwave is cooked before it is refrozen. [^3] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- Freeze leftovers within 3 to 4 days. [^4] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety)
- Never refreeze food that sat out of the fridge longer than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F). [^4] (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety)
- Wash hands at least 20 seconds with soap, and use one board for raw meat and another for produce. [^5] (https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html)
