# Gaps

## A starter set of everyday recipes with allergen-free and budget variants
I build meal plans and need real recipes to plan with. My book teaches how to adapt recipes but holds none (its brief: "recipe content itself is covered at the methodology/sourcing level, not as a recipe database"). About 30 everyday breakfasts, lunches, dinners and snacks from public-health sources such as USDA MyPlate Kitchen, Nutrition.gov, NHLBI's DASH recipes and the AHA. For each one: ingredients, steps, servings, nutrition per serving, which of the nine major US allergens it contains, and a swap for each. Mark which are DASH-shaped and which are cheap to make. No prices, please.

## Nutrient content of common foods
I turn a meal plan into protein, fiber, sodium, calcium and iron per day, and find foods high or low in a nutrient. My book names USDA FoodData Central but holds no food table. About 150 common foods from FoodData Central: a standard portion, then kcal, protein, carbohydrate, fiber, total and saturated fat, sodium, potassium, calcium, iron, vitamin D and B12 per portion. Group them by food type, and note which plant foods are fortified.

## The SCOFF eating-disorder questions and how to ask them kindly
I must screen for eating disorders before giving advice on weight or restriction. My book gives the SCOFF's accuracy and its rule (2 or more yes answers means referral) but not the five questions themselves. I need the five questions in their published wording, a plain-language version, how to raise them without alarming someone, and what to say on 2 or more yes answers, with the US referral resources and when to call emergency services.

## What a nutrition helper may remember about a person's health, and for how long
I keep a memory on one home computer of what a person tells me: allergies, conditions, medicines, goals, likes. The reading-room notes on my book say retention, minors and HIPAA-adjacency appear in no source. What do US privacy law, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state consumer-health-data laws (Washington's My Health My Data Act, for one) say about a local AI helper keeping that? Does it change for children or teens? What should I never keep, and what should I offer to forget?

## Nutrition guidance for people outside the US
My numbers come from US guidance (the DGA, the DRIs, USDA, FDA). The reading-room notes say no source covers other countries. For the UK, Canada, Australia and the EU: their national dietary guidelines, how their reference intakes differ from the US DRIs (protein, calcium, vitamin D, sodium, fiber), their allergen labelling law (the UK and EU list 14 allergens, the US 9), and each one's eating-disorder helpline.

## How long foods keep in the fridge and freezer
I answer "is this still good?" and plan batch cooking. My book says to use FoodSafety.gov's Cold Food Storage Chart rather than guess, but does not hold the chart, and I cannot open the web. I need that chart and the USDA FSIS freezing guidance as tables: fridge and freezer times for raw and cooked meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, deli foods, leftovers, soups, and cooked grains and beans. Note which freezer times are about quality and which about safety.

## What fruit and vegetables are in season, month by month
I tell people to buy produce in season to save money. My book points to Nutrition.gov's seasonal produce guide but does not hold it. I need a month-by-month US list of fruit and vegetables in season (SNAP-Ed or Nutrition.gov), how to store each one, and whether frozen or canned is a good swap. No prices, please.
