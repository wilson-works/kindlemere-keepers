# Vitamins and minerals, adults

Format: men 19-50 / women 19-50 / pregnancy / lactation, with the upper limit (UL) in brackets where one exists.

## Vitamins
- Vitamin A (µg RAE): 900 / 700 / 770 / 1,300 (UL 3,000). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:121
- Vitamin D (IU): 600 for all four (UL 4,000). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:122
- Vitamin E (mg): 15 / 15 / 15 / 19 (UL 1,000). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:123
- Vitamin K (µg): 120 / 90 / 90 / 90 (no UL set). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:124
- Vitamin C (mg): 90 / 75 / 85 / 120 (UL 2,000). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:125
- Thiamin (mg): 1.2 / 1.1 / 1.4 / 1.4. [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:126
- Riboflavin (mg): 1.3 / 1.1 / 1.4 / 1.6. [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:127
- Niacin (mg NE): 16 / 14 / 18 / 17 (UL 35). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:128
- Vitamin B6 (mg): 1.3 / 1.3 / 1.9 / 2.0 (UL 100). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:129
- Folate (µg DFE): 400 / 400 / 600 / 500 (UL 1,000). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:130
- Vitamin B12 (µg): 2.4 / 2.4 / 2.6 / 2.8 (no UL set). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:131
- Pantothenic acid (mg): 5 / 5 / 6 / 7. [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:132
- Biotin (µg): 30 / 30 / 30 / 35. [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:133
- Choline (mg): 550 / 425 / 450 / 550 (UL 3,500). [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:134
- Age shifts: vitamin D rises to 800 IU above 70; B6 rises to 1.7 mg (men) and 1.5 mg (women) from 51. [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:137
- The folate UL of 1,000 µg applies to synthetic folic acid from fortified food and supplements. [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:139

## Minerals
- Calcium (mg): 1,000 for all four (UL 2,500). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:145
- Iron (mg): 8 / 18 / 27 / 9-10 (UL 45). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:146
- Magnesium (mg): 400 / 310 (supplement UL 350). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:147
- Zinc (mg): 11 / 8 / 11 / 12 (UL 40). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:148
- Iodine (µg): 150 / 150 / 220 / 290 (UL 1,100). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:149
- Selenium (µg): 55 / 55 / 60 / 70 (UL 400). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:150
- Potassium (mg): 3,400 / 2,600 (no UL set). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:151
- Sodium: keep under 2,300 mg a day. [^5] [^10] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:152
- Copper (µg): 900 (UL 10,000). Phosphorus (mg): 700 (UL 4,000). [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:153
- Age shifts: calcium rises to 1,200 mg from 51 in women and 71 in men, and its UL drops to 2,000 mg at 51+; women's iron falls to 8 mg after menopause. [^8] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:157

## The upper-limit trap
- The magnesium UL (350 mg) is lower than the RDA because it counts only supplements and medicines, not magnesium in food. Food magnesium needs no limit in healthy people. [^90] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:161
- Full-source magnesium RDA: 400-420 mg for men, 310-320 mg for women. [^90] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:162
- Always say whether a UL covers supplements only (magnesium, folic acid, niacin, vitamin E) or total intake. Getting it backwards gives false alarms or false reassurance. [^90] [^7] @2026-10-07-nutritionist-agent/01-overview.md:166
