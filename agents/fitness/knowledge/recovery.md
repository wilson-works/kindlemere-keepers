# Soreness, recovery and rest

What DOMS is, which recovery methods help when, cold water and muscle growth, sleep, deloads, tapers, detraining, and overtraining.

## DOMS: what it is, and what it is not

- DOMS is local muscle tenderness, stiffness and pain arising 24 to 72 h after eccentric or high-intensity exercise. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:96
- It is self-limiting, peaks within 1 to 7 days, and usually resolves within a week. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:98
- DOMS does not come from lactic acid. That theory is now considered obsolete. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:100
- Current mechanisms: microtrauma and sarcomere disruption from eccentric loading, connective-tissue damage, an inflammatory cascade, nociceptor sensitisation (especially in fascia), and satellite cells in repair. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:102
- Peak soreness falls at 24 to 72 h. [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:106
- Soreness is not a measure of workout quality. It comes from unaccustomed eccentric loading. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:109

## Recovery methods: what helps, and when (22 RCTs)

- Jump performance at 24 h: active recovery ranked best, then cold-water immersion. [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:124
- Soreness right after: massage strongest; cold water also helped. At 24 h massage still ranked first. [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:125 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:126
- Soreness at 48 h: only cold-water immersion kept a significant benefit. [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:127
- Creatine kinase (muscle-damage marker): only cold water reduced it acutely. [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:128
- Overall, recovery effects depend on timing: active recovery for short-term power, massage for early soreness, cold water for broader short-term support. [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:130
- Active recovery and light activity repeatedly ease DOMS, which favours light movement over complete rest. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:133
- Foam rolling: benefits look mainly mechanical (tone, flexibility); pain relief is inconsistent. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:136
- Compression garments are endorsed for treatment and prevention of DOMS. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:138
- Static stretching for DOMS offers no meaningful clinical benefit. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:140
- Contrast baths add nothing over cold water alone. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:143
- Cryosauna at -110 °C, 48 to 72 h after exercise, reduced stiffness, leukocytes and creatine kinase. [^37] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:144

## Cold water and muscle growth

- Meta-analysis of 8 RCTs: resistance training alone gave more hypertrophy than training plus cold-water immersion. Small effect, 95.7% probability it favoured training alone. [^39] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:151 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:155
- If muscle growth is the priority, avoid cold water immediately after lifting. The finding applies to immediate post-exercise cooling. [^39] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:160
- Study quality was fair to poor, so the conclusion is directional, not settled. [^39] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:162
- Mechanism: cold water reduces mTORC1 signalling, shifting protein balance toward breakdown. [^40] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:164
- Over 12 weeks of twice-weekly training it has been reported to blunt hypertrophy and isometric strength gains. [^40] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:166
- Two uses of cold: acute soreness relief (supported) vs a routine habit after lifting when growth is the goal (a small cost). Not immediately after resistance sessions, and not by default. [^38] [^39] [^40] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:173

## Sleep

- Most elite athletes get less than the recommended 7 to 9 hours. [^41] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:183
- National Sleep Foundation: 7 to 9 hours for adults 18 to 64. [^41] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:185
- Sleep extension of 46 to 113 minutes is the proposed range for athletes who sleep about 7 hours. [^42] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:186
- Extension improved sprint times, shooting accuracy, tennis serve accuracy and endurance. [^41] [^42] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:188
- Naps of 20 to 90 minutes can improve performance and restore it after partial sleep loss. [^42] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:190
- Sleep is a primary recovery variable. [^43] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:192
- "Aim to add 45 to 60 minutes" is more useful than "sleep more". [^41] [^42] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:195
- Persistent unrefreshing sleep, loud snoring with witnessed pauses, or daytime sleepiness that extension does not fix: a medical referral, not coaching. [^12] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:195

## Rest days, deloads, tapers, breaks

- Deloads are commonly planned every 4 to 8 weeks. [^77] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:205
- Typical coach-prescribed deload week: 1 to 2 sessions, 30 to 60 min, 1 to 3 sets, 1 to 6 reps, 60 to 84% 1RM. [^45] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:206
- A 1-week deload in the middle of a 9-week programme hurt lower-body strength and did not help hypertrophy, power or endurance. [^44] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:208
- A taper before competition is different and helps: 2 to 8% better strength performance. [^77] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:213
- Under 7 days off is unlikely to cost strength. The drop becomes significant only after about the third week. [^46] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:215
- Tell apart a taper (supported), a routine mid-cycle deload (not supported for strength) and a short break (costs almost nothing). [^44] [^46] [^77] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:221
- Rest days remain because the guideline is 2 to 3 resistance days a week, not daily. [^4] [^7] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:221

## Overreaching and overtraining

- Functional overreaching: a short dip that turns into improvement after recovery. Normal training. [^12] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:141
- Non-functional overreaching: training and recovery out of balance; the dip persists. [^12] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:233
- Overtraining syndrome is told apart only by clinical outcome and exclusion. There is no confirmatory test. [^12] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:146
- No marker (hormonal, performance, psychological, biochemical, immune) meets accepted criteria. [^12] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:148
- Never label someone as overtrained. Describe the pattern (lasting performance drop, mood change, poor sleep despite less load) and refer to a physician: the differential includes anaemia, thyroid disease, depression and infection. [^12] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:151 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:238
- In-scope responses: cut volume rather than frequency, protect sleep, keep activity light rather than stopping, and escalate if performance and mood do not recover. [^12] [^37] [^41] [^42] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:241
