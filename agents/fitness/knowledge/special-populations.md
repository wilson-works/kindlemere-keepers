# Older adults, pregnancy, chronic conditions and injury

Guidance and cautions for older adults, pregnancy and postpartum, type 2 diabetes, hypertension and heart disease, arthritis, cancer survivors, disability, injury and concussion.

## Older adults

- CDC: 65+ need 150 min a week moderate (for example 30 min on 5 days), or 75 min vigorous, or a mix. [^60] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:156
- Plus 2+ days of muscle strengthening for legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms. [^60] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:158
- Plus balance activities each week, such as walking heel-to-toe and standing up from sitting. [^60] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:160
- If the targets are out of reach: be as active as abilities allow. Some activity beats none. [^60] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:162
- WHO: multicomponent balance and strength on 3+ days a week, strong recommendation. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:164
- Tai chi has high-certainty evidence for 20% fewer people who fall. [^50] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:168
- Uncontrolled hypertension contraindicates resistance training; unstable conditions need a physician. [^31] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:170
- Yoga injuries reaching emergency departments are more common at 65+. [^49] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:172

## Pregnancy and postpartum

Label: **CSEP-2019-only** for the contraindication lists. Louise could not open ACOG's or BMJ's own lists. ACOG supports only the general "safe and desirable" framing and the activities to avoid. [^62] [^63]

- WHO: at least 150 min a week moderate aerobic, with muscle strengthening. Strong recommendation. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:181
- PAG: pregnant and postpartum women follow the standard adult guidelines; activity reduces postpartum depression risk. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:184
- Canadian guideline (2019): 150+ min a week moderate over at least 3 days, daily activity encouraged; a variety of aerobic and resistance work; yoga or gentle stretching may help. [^62] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:186 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:189
- Pelvic floor muscle training (such as Kegels) may be done daily, with instruction in technique. [^62] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:191
- ACOG Committee Opinion 804 (2020): without complications or contraindications, activity in pregnancy is safe and desirable. [^63] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:193
- ACOG: people who did vigorous aerobic activity before pregnancy can continue. [^63] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:196
- ACOG's cited benefits: less gestational diabetes, caesarean birth and operative delivery, and quicker postpartum recovery. [^63] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:198
- Absolute contraindications (CSEP-2019-only): ruptured membranes; premature labour; unexplained persistent bleeding; placenta previa after 28 weeks; preeclampsia; incompetent cervix; growth restriction; triplets or more; uncontrolled type 1 diabetes, hypertension or thyroid disease; serious cardiovascular, respiratory or systemic disorders. [^62] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:186 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:203 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:204 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:205 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:206 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:207
- Relative contraindications, medical consultation needed (CSEP-2019-only): recurrent pregnancy loss; gestational hypertension; prior spontaneous preterm birth; mild or moderate heart or lung disease; symptomatic anaemia; malnutrition; eating disorder; twins after 28 weeks; other significant conditions. [^62] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:170 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:186 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:211 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:212 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:213
- Avoid (ACOG): contact sports, fall-risk activities, overheating, skydiving, scuba diving, and activity above 6,000 feet. [^63] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:218
- Avoid hot yoga and long periods lying on the back. [^49] [^62] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:220 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:222
- Default: confirm the person has discussed exercise with their obstetric provider, and never programme against a named contraindication. [^62] [^63] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:225

## Type 2 diabetes

- ACSM 2022 consensus updates the 2010 ACSM/ADA stand, broadening to physical activity generally and adding less sedentary time. [^69] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:237 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:239
- Be active regularly, reduce sitting, and break it up with frequent activity. Aerobic training improves glycaemic management. [^69] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:241 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:243
- Breaking sitting every 30 minutes (chair stands or short walks) is the operational habit. [^17] [^19] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:245

## Hypertension and heart disease

- Isometric exercise gave the largest resting blood-pressure drop (about 8.24/4.00 mmHg); wall squat ranked highest. [^33] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:252
- In cardiac rehabilitation, HIIT raised VO2peak more than steady training and was safe, in programmes over 12 weeks. [^21] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:255
- But known cardiac disease carries about 1 cardiac arrest per 60,000 training hours, so it belongs in supervised rehab, not AI-programmed training. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:257
- Uncontrolled hypertension contraindicates resistance training; resting BP over 200/110 is a relative contraindication. [^31] [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:260

## Arthritis

- Same mix: 150+ min moderate aerobic plus 2+ days of strengthening; older adults add balance. [^61] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:265 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:267
- People with arthritis are 2.5 times more likely to report two or more falls and a fall injury. More activity helps lower fall risk. [^61] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:269
- The American College of Rheumatology strongly recommends tai chi for knee and hip OA. [^50] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:272
- Modify resistance intensity and volume for osteoarthritis. [^31] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:274

## Cancer survivors (2019 roundtable consensus)

- Aerobic exercise 3 to 5 times a week for at least 30 min, plus resistance on 2 to 3 days. As a weekly dose: 150 min moderate or 75 vigorous. [^70] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:281 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:283
- Benefits: physical function, less fatigue, better quality of life, less anxiety and depressive symptoms. [^70] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:284
- Exercise can help prevent upper-arm lymphoedema from worsening. [^70] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:286
- Generally safe, but medical clearance is advised in certain situations, and survivors should avoid inactivity. [^70] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:287

## Disability and other conditions

- WHO extends its recommendations to people with disability (including MS, spinal cord injury, intellectual disability, Parkinson's, stroke, depression, schizophrenia, ADHD), with consultation for individual programming. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:292
- The PAG has adults with chronic conditions and disabilities follow adapted versions of the adult guidelines. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:295

## Injury and return to activity

- Diagnosing and treating an injury and designing its recovery is physical-therapist scope. [^74] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:304
- Prevention: strength training cuts sports injuries to under a third and overuse injuries by almost half; proprioceptive training about 45%; stretching no significant benefit. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:306
- The strongest running-load signal is a single session over 110% of the longest run in the last 30 days. [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:309
- Active recovery beats complete rest for soreness and short-term power. [^37] [^38] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:312
- "No pain, no gain" is not a rule. New or worsening joint pain is a referral. [^37] [^74] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:314

## Concussion (6th International Consensus, 2022)

- Strict rest is not beneficial. Relative rest (daily activities plus light, symptom-limited activity such as walking) may begin in the first 24 to 48 hours. [^71] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:322
- Reduced screen time in the first 48 hours is warranted. [^71] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:324
- After that, earlier return to activity and learning may help. [^71] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:326
- Progress with no more than mild symptoms: a rise of no more than 2 points on a 0 to 10 scale, lasting under an hour. [^71] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:328
- 93% of injured athletes returned fully to learning within 10 days without extra support. [^71] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:331
- A suspected concussion is an immediate referral and a hard stop on programming. The coach may describe the framework but never runs a return-to-play progression. [^71] [^74] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:334
