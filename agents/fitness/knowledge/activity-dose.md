# How much activity a week

Weekly activity targets by age and situation, and the dose-response numbers behind "is this enough?".

## The two guideline documents

- The US Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (2018), is the edition in force; it replaced the 2008 edition. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:14
- Adults: 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity, plus muscle-strengthening on at least 2 days a week. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:16
- Youth 6 to 17: at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily, including muscle- and bone-strengthening. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:18
- Preschoolers 3 to 5: active through the day, about 3 hours a day of active play. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:20
- The 2nd edition removed the 10-minute minimum bout. Any length of activity counts toward the weekly total. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:22
- Its framing is "sit less, move more": some activity is better than none. [^1] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:24
- WHO 2020 (the international counterpart) for adults 18 to 64: 150 to 300 min a week moderate, or 75 to 150 min vigorous, or a mix; muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days. Strong recommendation, moderate certainty. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:27 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:30
- Going above 300 min moderate or 150 min vigorous is a conditional recommendation, not a strong one. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:33
- Older adults 65+: the adult target plus multicomponent activity emphasising balance and strength on 3 or more days a week. Strong recommendation. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:35
- Children and adolescents 5 to 17: an average of 60 min a day moderate-to-vigorous, plus muscle- and bone-strengthening on 3 or more days. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:37
- Pregnant and postpartum women: at least 150 min a week moderate aerobic, including muscle strengthening. Strong recommendation. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:39
- Sedentary time: limit it and replace it with any activity. WHO could not quantify a sedentary threshold. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:41
- Adults with chronic conditions (hypertension, type 2 diabetes, cancer, HIV) and people with disability get the same targets, adjusted to ability. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:43
- Both documents converge, so "150 to 300 minutes a week plus two strength days" can be quoted without picking a side. WHO carries certainty ratings, which suit hedged language. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:46

## Dose-response

- 30 to 60 minutes a week of muscle-strengthening is associated with a 10 to 20% lower risk of all-cause, cardiovascular, diabetes and cancer mortality, independent of aerobic activity (16 studies). [^13] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:162
- That curve is J-shaped: no conclusive extra benefit beyond about an hour a week of muscle strengthening. [^13] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:166
- Steps and mortality (15 cohorts, 47,471 adults): risk levelled off at about 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day for adults 60+, and about 8,000 to 10,000 for adults under 60. [^14] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:168
- The highest step quartile had a 40 to 53% lower mortality risk than the lowest. [^14] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:171
- So the 10,000-step target is higher than older adults need for the mortality benefit. [^14] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:173
