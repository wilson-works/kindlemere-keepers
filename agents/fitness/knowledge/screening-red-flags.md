# Screening and red flags

The pre-participation screening questions, the stop-and-get-care symptoms, the contraindication lists, and cardiac risk in perspective.

## Screening before a plan (ACSM, 2015 revision)

- Three questions decide it: is the person currently exercising, do they have a history or symptoms of cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, and how hard do they want to train. [^64] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:51
- "Currently active" means planned, structured activity of at least 30 min at moderate intensity on at least 3 days a week for the past 3 months. [^64] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:54
- The 2015 revision deliberately reduced medical evaluation for healthy people without symptoms, so everyone is encouraged to be active. [^66] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:56
- Applied to US adults, it referred fewer people for clearance: 2.6% only before vigorous exercise, 54.2% before any exercise. [^64] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:59
- At minimum, screen with a self-guided questionnaire such as the PAR-Q or the AHA/ACSM facility questionnaire. [^64] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:62
- ACSM publishes an Exercise Preparticipation Health Screening Questionnaire through Exercise is Medicine. [^65] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:65
- The coach cannot do clinical screening, but it runs the questionnaire logic: known disease, symptoms, current activity, intended intensity. Then it routes to clearance where the algorithm says. [^64] [^65] [^66] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:68
- Default: someone currently inactive with any known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, or any suggestive symptom, is referred for clearance before starting. [^64] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:68

## Stop exercising and seek care

- Chest pain, pressure or discomfort, especially with exertion. Unstable angina is an absolute contraindication. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:85
- Fainting or near-fainting, dizziness, or loss of consciousness with exertion. [^64] [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:87
- Unusual or severe shortness of breath at rest or with light activity. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:88
- Palpitations or an irregular heartbeat. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:90
- Acute infection with fever, body aches or swollen glands: an absolute contraindication. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:92
- Resting blood pressure above 200/110 mmHg: a relative contraindication. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:94
- New or worsening joint pain, swelling, or inability to bear weight: a physical-therapy question. [^74] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:95
- Suspected concussion. [^71] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:97

## Absolute contraindications to exercise testing

- A recent ECG change suggesting ischaemia, or a heart attack within 2 days; unstable angina; uncontrolled symptomatic dysrhythmias; symptomatic severe aortic stenosis; uncontrolled symptomatic heart failure; acute pulmonary embolism; acute myocarditis or pericarditis; a suspected or known dissecting aneurysm; acute systemic infection with fever. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:88 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:92 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:101 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:105 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:107 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:109

## Relative contraindications

- Resting blood pressure over 200/110; tachy- or bradydysrhythmia; hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; high-degree AV block; ventricular aneurysm; uncontrolled metabolic disease; chronic infectious disease; impairment that prevents exercising adequately. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:94 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:117 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:119
- These can sometimes be outweighed by benefits, with caution, but that judgement is clinical, not coaching. [^68] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:122

## Cardiac risk in perspective (AHA 2020)

- Sudden cardiac death and heart attack risk rise briefly during and just after exercise. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:134
- 3- to 17-fold higher sudden death risk during and up to 30 min after vigorous exercise; 2- to 10-fold heart attack risk within an hour. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:134 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:136
- But absolute risk is small: 0.31 to 2.1 exertion-related sudden deaths per 100,000 a year; 0.3 under 35, 3.0 over 35. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:138
- Non-exertional sudden cardiac death runs at 43 to 55 per 100,000 a year. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:134 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:140
- With known cardiac disease: about 1 cardiac arrest per 60,000 hours of aerobic training. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:141
- Risk is higher when effort is strenuous, sudden, unaccustomed or heavily anaerobic. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:143
- Regular activity lowers the transient risk: relative risk 9.0 for women doing under 2 h a week vs 1.5 for 2+ h. [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:144
- The central safety habit: progress gradually and avoid sudden unaccustomed vigorous effort, especially for previously inactive people. [^64] [^67] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:149
