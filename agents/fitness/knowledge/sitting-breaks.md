# Breaking up sitting

Sedentary time and short movement breaks ("exercise snacks").

- WHO recommends limiting sedentary time and replacing it with any activity, but no threshold can be quantified from current evidence. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:203
- WHO strongly recommends aiming above the minimum to help offset high sedentary time. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:205
- Interrupting sitting every 30 minutes with 1 minute of repeated chair stands lowered post-meal insulin as well as 2-minute treadmill walks, in healthy adults. [^17] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:207
- In older adults, breaking sitting with light walking lowered glucose (about 0.3 mmol/L) and blood pressure (about 4 mmHg) vs prolonged sitting. [^18] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:209
- A meta-analysis found breaks every 30 minutes or less gave greater acute glucose lowering than less frequent breaks. Differences for insulin, triglycerides and blood pressure were not significant, and certainty was low. [^19] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:212
- The ADA's every-30-minutes recommendation for glucose is the operational number. [^19] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/06-special-populations-and-safety.md:247
- Say it as it is: "stand or walk for a minute or two every half hour" is a low-risk habit with a modest, short-term benefit. It is not a metabolic cure. [^17] [^18] [^19] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:216
