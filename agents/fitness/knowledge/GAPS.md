# Gaps

## The ACSM 2026 resistance training position stand itself: second sources for its five headline numbers
The fitness coach quotes five numbers from ACSM's 2026 stand (Currier et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026;58(4):851-872): 80% 1RM or more for 2 to 3 sets for strength, about 10 sets per muscle a week for growth, every muscle twice a week, failure optional, equipment largely irrelevant. Louise's book could open only ACSM's announcement and press coverage, so the coach labels them "asserted". The paper itself, or a second source that can be read, for each number would let it drop the label. (Council action F1, 2026-10-07.)

## ACOG Committee Opinion 804: its own list of contraindications to exercise in pregnancy
The coach's pregnancy contraindication lists come only from the 2019 Canadian (CSEP) guideline, because ACOG's and BMJ's pages could not be opened. ACOG's absolute and relative contraindications, from a source that can be read, would let the coach check the two lists agree before it refers anyone. (Council action F2, 2026-10-07.)

## Exercise and crisis resources for people outside the United States
Every resource in the coach's book is a US instrument: the Physical Activity Guidelines, CDC, ACSM, and the 988 crisis line. A person in another country who reaches a crisis moment, or asks which guideline applies to them, gets nothing local. What would answer it: the main national activity guidelines and the crisis lines for the countries people most often write from, with how to find a local line anywhere.

## A movement library: how to do each basic strength pattern, with easier and harder versions
The coach plans strength work by pattern (squat, hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push or pull, core anti-extension) but its book names no exercises and gives no technique cues. What would answer it: for each pattern, a few named exercises with bodyweight, band, dumbbell and machine versions, the key form cues a trainer gives, and the usual easier and harder steps.

## Named stretches for each major muscle-tendon group
The coach knows the dose (about 60 seconds per muscle-tendon group, 2 or more days a week; 5 minutes a week per group for long-term range) but not the stretches. What would answer it: one or two named static and dynamic stretches for each major group, how to do them, and common mistakes, from an exercise-science or physical-therapy source.

## Guided scripts for breath awareness, body scan and slow paced breathing
The coach may teach slow paced breathing with a longer exhale and the core MBSR practices, but its book gives no breathing rate, ratio or session script. What would answer it: the paced-breathing rates and exhale ratios used in the trials, and short, evidence-based scripts for breath awareness and a body scan, with their safety notes.

## What a fitness coach agent may keep in memory about a person's health
The coach remembers what a person tells it (injuries, conditions, pregnancy, mood) on their own computer. Its book holds nothing on how long to keep health details, what to do for minors, or privacy expectations for a coaching tool that is not a medical provider. (Raised by the council, 2026-10-07.) What would answer it: plain guidance for a non-clinical wellness tool on keeping, forgetting and protecting health information.

## Postpartum return to exercise: when and how to build back up
The book says postpartum women should reach 150 minutes a week and that activity lowers postpartum depression risk, but gives no timeline or stages for returning after birth, including after a caesarean. What would answer it: the ACOG, CSEP or similar guidance on when to start, what to start with, and the warning signs that mean stop.

## Exercising in heat, cold, rain and wind, and at altitude
The book covers when not to exercise for medical reasons but not the weather. What would answer it: the signs of heat illness and cold injury during exercise, how to adjust a run or a plan for heat, cold, rain, wind and altitude (pace, length, what to wear, when to move it indoors), and when to stop. Huff answers running in the heat and Puff running in the weather, so both wait on this. (Drinking amounts belong to the nutritionist.)

## Training plans for cycling, swimming and rowing
The book builds running plans from evidence but has nothing specific for other common endurance activities. What would answer it: how to start and progress cycling, swimming and rowing for a general adult, the common overuse injuries in each, and how weekly minutes translate.
