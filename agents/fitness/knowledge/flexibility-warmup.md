# Stretching and warming up

How much to stretch, which technique, static stretching before effort, and the RAMP warm-up.

## The flexibility prescription

- ACSM 2011: stretches for each major muscle-tendon group, 60 seconds per exercise in total, on 2+ days a week. [^4] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:11
- Static, PNF and ballistic stretching are all named as acceptable. [^4] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:13
- Neuromotor work (balance, agility, coordination, gait) is separate, on 2 to 3 days a week. [^4] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:15
- WHO folds balance into multicomponent activity on 3+ days a week for older adults. [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:17

## Long-term range of motion

- Meta-analysis of 23 studies: every stretching type improved long-term range of motion. Static gave significantly greater gains than ballistic or PNF. [^36] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:22
- It suggested at least 5 minutes a week per muscle group of static stretching, and that 5+ days a week may help. [^36] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:25

## Right now: technique barely matters

- Behm et al. 2023: no significant difference between techniques for acute range of motion. [^34] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:22 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:31
- Stretching beat no stretching with a small effect. [^34] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:35
- No relationship between hold length and gain. Holds from 5 to 900 seconds all helped. [^34] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:36
- All types can be used with similar results, regardless of intensity, duration, technique, sex, age or training status. [^34] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:39
- So prescribe total weekly time and consistency, not a hold length or technique. [^34] [^36] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:43

## Static stretching before effort: the dose is the problem

- Short static stretching (60 s or less per muscle group) inside a full warm-up trivially impairs strength and power: about 1 to 2%. [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:51
- Longer (over 60 s per muscle group) gives practical declines of 4.0 to 7.5%. [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:54
- One meta-analysis (104 studies): maximal strength down 5.4%, power down 1.9%. [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:56
- Another (125 studies): 60 s or more per muscle group, 4.6% decline; 60 s or less, 1.1%. [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:58
- Put short static stretches inside a warm-up that also has aerobic activity, dynamic stretching and sport-specific work. [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:60
- Stretching showed no significant injury-prevention benefit. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:64
- The honest line: a quick static stretch before training is not harmful; holding over a minute per muscle right before a maximal effort reduces performance; stretching is not injury prevention. [^26] [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:67

## The RAMP warm-up

- Four phases: Raise, Activate, Mobilise, Potentiate. [^47] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:75
- Raise: low-intensity activity lifts body temperature, heart rate, breathing and blood flow. [^47] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:76
- Activate and Mobilise: dynamic movement is favoured over static holds. [^47] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:78
- Potentiate: progressively harder, task-specific work. [^47] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:80
- In youth soccer players RAMP beat static stretching or no warm-up for sprint and jump performance. [^47] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:82
- It matches the advice that a warm-up has an aerobic part, dynamic stretching and sport-specific activity. [^35] [^47] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/04-mobility-flexibility-recovery.md:60
