# How hard should it feel

Measuring intensity in practice: the talk test, heart-rate zones, RPE, METs, and the honest Zone 2 caveat.

## Everyday measures

- Talk test (CDC): at moderate intensity you can talk but not sing. At vigorous intensity you cannot say more than a few words without pausing for breath. [^15] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:182
- Moderate examples (CDC): brisk walking at 3 mph or faster, water aerobics, cycling under 10 mph on the flat, doubles tennis, ballroom dancing, general gardening. [^15] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:184
- Vigorous examples (CDC): race walking, jogging or running, swimming laps, singles tennis, aerobic dancing, cycling 10 mph or faster or with hills, jumping rope, continuous digging, hiking uphill or with a heavy pack. [^15] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:187
- Estimated maximum heart rate is 220 minus age. A 25-year-old's estimate is 195 bpm. [^16] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:190
- Zones against that estimate: light under 64% HRmax, moderate 64 to 76%, vigorous 77 to 93%. [^16] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:191
- Worked example: a 25-year-old at moderate intensity aims for roughly 125 to 148 bpm. [^16] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:193
- Consumer 5-zone watches: zones 2 to 3 usually match moderate and zone 4 vigorous. The zones can be reset to match the CDC bands. [^16] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:194
- 220 minus age is a population estimate, not a measurement. RPE and the talk test are often the more honest instruction for an untested beginner. [^3] [^15] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:196
- METs: moderate is 3 to 5.9, vigorous is 6.0 or more. [^3] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:29
- RPE on the Borg 6 to 20 scale: moderate about 12 to 13, vigorous about 14 or more. [^3] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:30

## Zone 2, and the honest caveat

- Zone 2 is usually placed at or just below the first lactate threshold, often taken as blood lactate below about 2.0 mmol/L. [^22] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:34
- Markers in use include 72 to 82% HRmax, 1.5 to 2.5 mmol/L lactate, baseline lactate plus 0.5 mmol/L, ventilatory threshold 1, 65% of peak VO2, and maximal fat-oxidation intensity. [^22] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:36
- In 50 experienced cyclists these markers did not agree. Coefficients of variation ran from 6% to 29%. [^22] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:39
- The authors conclude that percentage-based markers may not produce consistent responses across individuals. A fixed percentage can land someone too low or too high. [^22] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:42
- So present Zone 2 as easy, conversational, sustainable for an hour. Treat any HR percentage as an estimate. Never tell someone their watch's Zone 2 is physiologically right for them. [^22] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:46
