# Overload, progression and periodization

How a plan advances, what specificity and detraining mean, and how much periodization matters.

## Progression

- Overload: adaptation needs a stimulus greater than the body is used to, so the programme must advance. [^3] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:93
- Progression must be gradual. Advancing too quickly is the main avoidable risk in exercise prescription. [^3] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:95
- The concrete rule (ACSM 2009): increase load by 2 to 10% once the person can do 1 to 2 reps beyond the target on two consecutive sessions. [^5] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:97
- Specificity: adaptations are specific to the mode, muscles, velocity and energy systems trained. That is why the four components are prescribed separately. [^3] [^4] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:100
- Detraining: maximal force does not drop measurably in under 7 days off. The drop becomes significant only after about the third week. [^46] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:103

## Periodization: real, but smaller than the industry implies

- Meta-analysis (81 effects, 18 studies, 1988 to 2015): periodized resistance training gave greater 1RM gains than non-periodized, effect size 0.43. [^10] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:111
- That analysis found publication bias. Removing the outsized studies roughly halved the effect, though it stayed significant. [^10] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:113
- Gains were greater in untrained people, and with higher frequency and longer duration. The advantage is partly a proxy for training more and longer. [^10] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:115
- Linear periodization: high volume and low intensity moving to low volume and high intensity over months. [^11] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:118
- Undulating periodization: loading varies daily, weekly or every two weeks. [^11] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:120
- Linear vs undulating for strength: one meta-analysis found no difference; later ones suggested undulating gave greater maximal strength. [^11] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:121
- Linear vs undulating for hypertrophy: two meta-analyses found no advantage to either. [^11] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:123
- Whether periodized beats non-periodized for hypertrophy specifically is unclear. Most studies are short and rarely include tapers. [^11] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:124 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:126
- For general-population clients, periodization is an optional organising device, not a requirement. A beginner does not need a mesocycle chart. [^8] [^9] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/01-exercise-science-foundations.md:129
