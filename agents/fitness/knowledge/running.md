# Running load, injury and a first 5k

How fast to build running, what actually prevents running injuries, and a couch-to-5k style template built from the evidence.

## The real signal is the single session, not the week

- Best current evidence: the Garmin-RUNSAFE Running Health Study (BJSM 2025). [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:119
- 5,205 runners (mean age 45.8, 22% female); 1,820 (35%) had a running-related injury across 588,071 sessions. [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:122
- Overuse injury rose when a single session exceeded 110% of the longest run in the previous 30 days. [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:124
- Hazard rate ratios vs staying at or under +10%: small spike (over 10 to 30%) 1.64; moderate (over 30 to 100%) 1.52; large (over 100%) 2.28. [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:126
- The week-to-week ratio showed no relationship with injury. The acute:chronic workload ratio showed a negative dose-response. [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:128
- Earlier: a 13-week programme at about +10% a week showed no difference in injuries from an 8-week programme at about +24% a week. [^25] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:130
- So replace "no more than 10% more mileage a week" with "keep any single run within about 10% of your longest run in the last month." [^24] [^25] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:134
- For questions about whether someone's own habits are a risk factor, the companion RUNSAFE analysis of self-reported training characteristics is the source. [^29] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:140 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:142

## What prevents injuries

- Lauersen et al. (BJSM 2014), a meta-analysis of RCTs on injury prevention. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:147
- Strength training cut sports injuries to less than a third of control levels, and overuse injuries by almost half. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:149
- Proprioceptive training cut injury risk by roughly 45%. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:151
- Stretching showed no significant injury-prevention benefit. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:152
- For a runner asking how to stay healthy, the first answer is two strength sessions a week and conservative single-session jumps, not stretching. [^24] [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:156
- Training parameters are consistently implicated in running injuries, though that evidence base was limited. [^25] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:161
- Biomechanical risk factors call for injury-specific analysis, so no single gait or cadence cue can be offered as general injury prevention. [^28] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:163 @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:165

## A first-5k template (a template, not a prescription for one person)

- Weeks 1 to 3, frequency first: 3 sessions a week of walk/run intervals, 20 to 30 min a session, run segments at talk-test moderate. [^1] [^15] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:175
- Progression rule: lengthen run segments so no single session exceeds about 110% of the longest continuous run in the last 30 days. [^24] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:178
- Strength from week 1: two sessions a week for hips, glutes, quadriceps and calves. [^26] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:180
- Weeks 4 to 8, consolidate: keep 3 runs a week. One can grow toward 30 continuous minutes while the others stay short. Weekly moderate minutes should pass 150 around here. [^1] [^2] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:182
- Weeks 9 to 12: add one faster session (intervals or tempo). About 11 min of interval work a session captures 80% of the achievable VO2max effect. [^20] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:185
- Do not add static stretching as a warm-up fix. It does not reduce injury, and long holds impair the next effort. [^26] [^35] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:188
- Step-back weeks reduce volume rather than stopping. Strength is not meaningfully lost in under 7 days. [^46] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:190
- Say plainly that this is a template, and screen first. [^64] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:172

## Cardio for health outcomes (training side)

- Aerobic training improves glycaemic management in type 2 diabetes (ACSM 2022). [^69] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:199
- Isometric exercise gave the largest resting blood-pressure drop of any mode (270 RCTs): about 8.24 mmHg systolic, 4.00 diastolic. The wall squat ranked highest. [^33] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:201
- Walking or jogging had the largest antidepressant effect of any mode in a 218-study network meta-analysis (Hedges' g -0.63). [^53] @2026-10-07-health-and-fitness-agent/02-cardio-and-running.md:204
