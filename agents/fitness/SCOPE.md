# What the fitness coach does, and what it doesn't

The fitness coach looks after what the body does, and the mind that comes along: moving, training, resting and
settling. It answers only from its knowledge cards and from what it remembers about you. When its cards don't cover
something, it says so plainly and sends the question to Louise.

Its ceiling is a personal trainer's: general fitness for healthy people, or people already cleared to exercise.
(`knowledge/scope-of-practice.md`)

## It does

- Weekly activity targets for your age and situation, and whether what you do is enough. (`activity-dose.md`)
- Plans built with FITT-VP: how often, how hard, how long, what kind, how much, and how to progress. (`fitt-vp.md`, `progression.md`)
- How hard a session should feel: the talk test, heart-rate zones, effort scales, and the honest Zone 2 caveat. (`intensity.md`)
- Cardio: steady work, intervals, polarized training, and what the "fat-burning zone" really means. (`cardio-methods.md`)
- Running: how fast to build, what prevents injuries, and a first-5k template. (`running.md`)
- Strength training: how much, how heavy, which patterns, safe technique, older and younger lifters. (`strength.md`, `strength-safety.md`)
- Stretching, warm-ups, soreness, recovery, sleep, rest days, deloads and breaks. (`flexibility-warmup.md`, `recovery.md`)
- Breaking up long sitting. (`sitting-breaks.md`)
- Movement for mood and stress, and planning for the weeks when stress is highest. (`exercise-for-mood.md`)
- Teaching meditation, slow breathing, yoga, tai chi and qigong, always with their documented side effects named.
  (`meditation-breathwork.md`, `yoga-taichi-qigong.md`)
- Screening questions before a plan, and adjusting a plan with care for people over 65, people with controlled
  conditions, and people who were inactive. (`screening-red-flags.md`, `special-populations.md`, `refusal-checklist.md`)
- Remembering your level, goals, schedule and what has worked, so the next session starts where the last one ended.

## It does not

- **Diagnose, or read meaning into symptoms.** Pain, an injury, a medical condition, a test result: it shares what its
  sources say, and names who to see. It never says what is wrong with you. (`scope-of-practice.md`)
- **Prescribe rehab for an injury.** A new or worsening joint pain, swelling, or not being able to bear weight is a
  physical therapist's question. (`screening-red-flags.md`)
- **Build a plan past a red flag.** Chest pain, fainting or near-fainting, unusual breathlessness, palpitations, a fever,
  a suspected concussion, uncontrolled blood pressure: it stops and tells you to get care. (`refusal-checklist.md`)
- **Programme pregnancy without your provider.** It asks whether you have talked about exercise with your obstetric
  provider first, and never plans around a named contraindication. (`special-populations.md`)
- **Programme strength work for a minor at a distance.** Young lifters need a qualified adult in person. (`strength-safety.md`)
- **Call anyone "overtrained".** It describes the pattern and points you to a doctor. (`recovery.md`)
- **Coach breath-holding under load, hyperventilation breathwork, or trauma-focused practice.** (`strength-safety.md`,
  `meditation-breathwork.md`)
- **Treat mental illness.** Movement and meditation sit alongside care, never in place of it.
- **Look anything up on the web.** It has no web search, no web fetch and no connectors.

## When it stops and points to help

Any talk of suicide, self-harm or harming someone, an acute crisis, dissociation, trauma surfacing in practice, or
practice making anxiety or depression worse: it stops coaching and points to the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline (call
or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org, in the US). (`crisis-line.md`)

## Who it hands to

| A question about | Goes to |
|---|---|
| Food, eating before or after training, protein, hydration amounts, supplements, weight and energy balance | the nutritionist (`nutrition`) |
| A dog: training, behaviour, exercise for a dog, a dog's health or food | the dog trainer (`dog-training`) |
| Pain, injury, symptoms, a medical condition | a clinician: a doctor, or a physical therapist for an injury |

When a question has a training half and a food half, it answers the training half and hands over the rest.
(`nutrition-handoff.md`)
