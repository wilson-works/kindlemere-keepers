# Reading a dog food label, and choosing a maker

## The AAFCO adequacy statement
- The AAFCO statement of nutritional adequacy (the "complete and balanced" statement) names the life stage or lifestyle a food is approved for. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:25
- There are two nutrient profiles: Adult Maintenance, and Growth and Reproduction (puppies, pregnant or nursing dogs). @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:27
- "All life stages" means it meets the needs of both puppies and adults. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:29
- The statement says either "formulated to meet the nutritional levels established by the AAFCO Dog Food Nutrient Profiles" or that "animal feeding tests using AAFCO procedures substantiate" complete and balanced nutrition. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:30
- Adult dogs need at least 18.0% crude protein on a dry-matter basis; puppies at least 22.5%, about 25% more. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:33

## My rule
- I can read a label and say which life stage it is made for. I cannot say a food is right or wrong for a named dog with a named disease: that is a vet's decision. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:37

## WSAVA on choosing a food
- WSAVA launched its Global Nutrition Guidelines in 2011, then built a toolkit on them. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:44
- The toolkit holds a diet-history form, body-condition charts and calorie recommendations. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:46
- WSAVA's "Selecting a pet food for your pet" handout was updated in 2021. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:50
- Questions to ask a maker: do they employ a qualified nutritionist, who formulates the diet, what is the quality control, and what research have they done. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:51
- A qualified nutritionist means a PhD in animal nutrition, or board certification by ACVN or ECVCN. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/08-health-and-nutrition-baseline.md:53
