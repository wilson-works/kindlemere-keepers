# Gaps

## The first night after a dog bite: what a handler should do
My book covers referral after a bite (a vet plus a veterinary behaviourist) but not the handler's first questions that night: whether a bite must be reported and to whom, rabies-exposure steps, wound first aid, and who to call tonight. Louise's own summary card names this gap. US first, with other countries named where they differ.

## House-training and crate training a puppy or a rescue dog
My book lists "housetrained" as a CGCU test item but gives no method. I need the evidence-backed method for toilet training and crate introduction across ages, what welfare bodies say about time in a crate, and common mistakes.

## Puppy mouthing, nipping and jumping up
My book covers the socialisation window but not these everyday puppy behaviours. I need reward-based methods with evidence, what is normal at each age, and where mouthing tips into a referral.

## How to teach loose-lead walking
My book names loose-lead walking as a CGC item and rules out prong and choke collars, but has no teaching method. I need the reward-based methods, what the evidence says about harnesses against flat collars and head halters, and how to raise criteria.

## Profiles of highly intelligent, high-drive breeds beyond the Border Collie
My book covers the seven AKC groups and the Border Collie in depth. I need the same depth, with breed-as-a-hint caveats, for the breeds people most often call "too smart for me": Australian Shepherd, Belgian Malinois, German Shepherd, Standard Poodle, and herding and working mixes. Temperament data such as C-BARQ, typical jobs, and common under-stimulation problems.

## Foods and household items that are toxic to dogs
I use food as a training reward, and my book has no list of foods that are dangerous to dogs. I need a veterinary-sourced list and what to do if a dog eats one, framed as "call a vet or a pet poison line", never a home treatment.

## Bringing home a rescue or adult dog: the first weeks
My book covers puppies and life stages but not adopting an adult or rescue dog. I need what the evidence says about the first days and weeks at home, settling in, when to start training, and introducing a new dog to a resident dog.

## Preparing a dog for a new baby or a child in the home
My book says never leave children unsupervised with dogs, and refers homes with a reactive dog and a child. I need the preventive side: preparing a calm dog for a baby, teaching children to read dog body language, and household routines.

## Dog training standards outside the United States
All my instruments are US ones (AKC CGC, AAFCO, AAHA). Louise's reading room named anything outside the United States as a gap. I need the equivalent good-citizen tests, food-label rules and veterinary bodies in the UK, Canada, Australia and the EU.

## What a dog coach should keep about a dog's bite history, and for how long
I remember each dog on the person's own computer, including behaviour history. Louise's reading room found that record retention, minors and a stored bite history appear in no source. I need what trainers' and behaviourists' professional codes say about keeping client and dog records, and any legal angle on keeping a bite record.
