# Attachment, the bond, and why method changes the relationship

## Dogs attach the way infants do
- Topál et al. (1998) applied Ainsworth's Strange Situation Test to dogs and documented attachment behaviour toward owners. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:11
- Palmer and Custance (2008) found a secure-base effect: dogs explored and played more with their owner present than with a stranger or alone. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:13
- Solomon et al. (2019) adapted the classification procedures too, so the field now has attachment categories. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:19
- So "the dog needs to respect you" is not the mechanism. Whether the handler works as a secure base is, and it shows in exploring and play. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:25

## Attachment changes training results
- Adolescent cue failure was stronger in dogs with less secure attachment, and it was carer-specific. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:32
- Relationship work is not a soft extra: it changes cue reliability at a measurable age. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:40

## The physical side of the bond
- Nagasawa et al. (2015, Science): a dog's gaze, but not a tamed wolf's, raised the owner's oxytocin, which raised the dog's. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:47
- Long mutual gazes raised oxytocin in both; short ones left it stable. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:49
- Coaching use: voluntary eye contact is a trainable attention behaviour and a bonding event in one act. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:56

## Method shapes the relationship
- Rooney and Cowan (2011): dogs of more playful, more patient owners did better at a novel task. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:63
- Owners who used punishment had dogs that were less playful and interacted less with a stranger. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:65
- Studies cited by AVSAB: aversive-trained dogs showed stress during training; reward-trained dogs were more attentive to their owner. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:68
- AVSAB names damage to the human-animal bond as an effect of aversive training. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:72

## Mechanics and relationship are both required
- Reinforcement contingencies and marker timing change behaviour. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:148
- But mechanics alone are not enough: attachment security moderated adolescent cue reliability. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:150
- Handler playfulness and patience mattered apart from technique. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:152
- I refuse the framing that one replaces the other. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/07-trust-and-relationship.md:161
