# What a breed label tells you, and what it does not

## The headline
- Breed explains roughly 9% of behavioural variation between individual dogs (Morrill et al. 2022, Science; 18,385 dogs surveyed, 2,155 sequenced). @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:10
- Most behavioural traits are over 25% heritable, but behaviour only subtly separates breeds. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:12
- Human sociability was the most heritable factor measured (67.3 ± 13.0%); retrieving was the most heritable single trait (52.5 ± 9.2%). @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:13
- Physical traits were over five times more likely than behavioural traits to be predicted by breed. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:15
- Agonistic threshold (how easily a dog is provoked) is almost uninformative from breed. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:16
- Age and sex predicted some specific behaviours better than breed did. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:18
- Eleven loci were strongly associated with behaviour, and none were breed-specific. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/01-overview.md:41

## My rule
- Breed is a working hypothesis about what the dog may find rewarding and how much work it may want. It says nothing reliable about aggression, fear or sociability in a named dog. Ask about the dog in front of the handler. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:20 @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/01-overview.md:43

## The seven AKC groups, as a hint about the original job
- Herding (33 breeds): Bred to control animals' movement; described as intelligent; often herd family members, children included. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:32
- Sporting (33): Pointers, retrievers, setters, spaniels; active and alert; need regular vigorous exercise. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:33
- Working (32): Guarding, sled-pulling, water rescue; capable, and need proper training given size and strength. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:34
- Hound (32): Hunt by scent on a trail or by stamina in pursuit; many are vocal. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:35
- Terrier (31): Bred to hunt vermin; feisty, energetic, limited tolerance for other animals. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:36
- Toy (23): Companions, apartment-suited, tough temperaments despite size. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:37
- Non-Sporting (20): A mixed residual group (Poodle, Dalmatian, French Bulldog) with no shared behaviour signature: treat as no prior. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:38

## Crossbreeds and "designer" dogs
- The claim that Poodle crosses are easier and suit families better is not supported. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:114
- Bryson et al. (PLOS ONE, 2026) compared 9,402 UK dogs by C-BARQ: Cockapoos, Cavapoos and Labradoodles against their purebred parent breeds. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:116
- The crosses showed more undesirable behaviour than a parent breed in 44.4% of comparisons and fewer in 9.7%. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:118
- Cockapoos scored worse in 16 of 24 behaviours, Cavapoos in 11 of 24; Labradoodles were worse in 5 and better in 6. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:120
- All three showed more non-social fear, more separation problems and more excitability than their non-Poodle parent breed. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:124
- In mixed dogs, ancestry predicted behaviour only weakly (biddability the partial exception), so assess the dog directly rather than reasoning from a DNA panel. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/02-breeds-and-temperament.md:131

## Breed and the law (contested, both sides named)
- AVSAB's July 2014 position holds breed-specific legislation ineffective and supports education-based, breed-neutral dangerous-dog law. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/01-overview.md:120
- AVMA policy likewise supports dangerous-animal law only where it does not name breeds. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/01-overview.md:124
