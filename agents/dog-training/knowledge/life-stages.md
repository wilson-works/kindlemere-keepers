# Puppy, adolescent, adult and senior

## The quick table
- Neonatal and transitional. Age window: 0 to 3 weeks. Main training goal: Breeder-side handling only; the social bonding window has not opened @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:166
- Socialisation. Age window: 3 to 12 weeks, AVSAB stress on the first 3 months. Main training goal: The most safe, positive novelty possible; class from 7 to 8 weeks after one vaccine set @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:167
- Adolescence. Age window: About 6 to 18 months, conflict peak near 8 months. Main training goal: Hold criteria low, protect the relationship, manage the environment @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:168
- Adult. Age window: About 1 to 7 years. Main training goal: Generalisation, duration, distraction, sport and scent; 1 to 2 sessions a week is enough for simple tasks @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:169
- Senior. Age window: Last 25% of estimated lifespan. Main training goal: Adapted cognitive enrichment and a low threshold for vet referral @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:170

## Puppies: the socialisation window
- AVSAB: the first three months are the primary and most important time for socialisation. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:23
- Expose the puppy to as many new people, animals, sounds and places as can be managed safely, without overstimulating. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:25
- AVSAB holds that socialising before full vaccination should be the standard of care. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:27
- Puppy class can start at 7 to 8 weeks, with one set of vaccinations, no signs of illness, and follow-up vaccines kept current. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:31
- Behaviour problems, not infectious disease, are cited as the number one cause of death for dogs under three. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:37
- Caveat to carry: it is a sensitive period, not an absolute critical one; breed differences in when fear-avoidance starts (4 to 10 weeks) make one universal cut-off hard to defend. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:42
- Owners who skipped puppy class had 1.5 times the odds of a problem behaviour at 9 months. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:49
- Dogs trained both as puppies and as adults responded better to cues than untrained dogs or "puppy party" dogs (Kutsumi et al. 2013, 142 dogs). @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:53
- Honest framing: classes are one factor among many, not a guarantee. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:57
- Coach in this window: positive exposures short of overstimulation, novelty paired with food, handling foundations, name response and hand-feeding. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:62

## Adolescence: the "forgetting" phase
- Asher et al. (2020) documented a passing phase of carer-specific conflict-like behaviour in adolescence: reduced response to cues. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:75
- Dogs responded less to "sit" only when their carer asked, not a stranger. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:77
- The dip peaked around eight months, and was stronger in dogs with less secure attachment. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:79
- So: tell the handler it is documented and passing, not a failure or a dominance challenge. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:89
- Do not escalate to corrections; reward-based methods apply to all training, and confrontational handling raises aggressive-response risk. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:90
- Work on the attachment relationship alongside, and expect this to be the peak surrender window. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:93
- A "second fear period" around 6 to 14 months is convention, not finding. Label it that way. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:97

## Adults
- AAHA's 2019 Canine Life Stage Guidelines split young adult from mature adult, each with its own preventive-care checklist. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:105
- One session once or twice a week gave the best acquisition of a simple task; three back-to-back daily sessions the worst; retention was the same for all at four weeks (Demant et al. 2011). @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:110
- Any new behaviour problem in a previously stable adult is a medical question first. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:116

## Seniors
- Senior means the last 25% of estimated lifespan, so no fixed age applies. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:125
- Canine cognitive dysfunction (CCD) estimates range 14 to 67% in seniors; one synthesis says 14 to 35% over eight years and about 70% beyond 15. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:133
- Structured play, training and problem-solving support cognitive resilience in ageing dogs. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:139
- May: shorter sessions, high-value food, scent and foraging over physical drilling, predictable routine, continued learning. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:148
- Must not diagnose CCD. Night waking, disorientation, new house-soiling or changed social behaviour go to a vet. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:152
- Must not put a new behaviour change down to "just getting old", and must re-check pain. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/03-developmental-stages.md:155
