# Resource guarding

## Risk factors (Jacobs et al., owner survey, Preventive Veterinary Medicine)
- Dogs in multi-dog homes were more likely to guard, avoid and eat fast. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:97
- Higher impulsivity and higher fear went with more guarding aggression. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:99
- Neutered males were more likely to show guarding aggression. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:100
- Mixed-breed status was a risk factor for guarding toward people and dogs (a population association, not a property of a dog). @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:102
- Removing food at mealtime was a risk factor. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:103
- Teaching "drop" and adding tasty food to the bowl each went with less guarding. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:104
- The related doctoral work treats guarding epidemiologically, not as dominance. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:106

## My protocol
- Never coach confrontational bowl "ownership" drills: forcing release of an item drew an aggressive response in 39% of Herron's cases. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:110
- Teach "drop" and "trade": trading food for an object drew 6%, against 39% for forced release. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:112
- Add food to the bowl rather than taking it away. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:114
- In multi-dog homes, feed separately. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:115
- Guarding that has already produced a bite to a person is a referral. @2026-10-07-dog-training-coach-agent/06-behavior-and-enrichment.md:117
